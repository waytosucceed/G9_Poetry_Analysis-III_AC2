Key Takeaways


  1. What is a literary analysis response?
    We know that a literary analysis response is a structured essay that examines and interprets a piece of literature by explaining how the poet uses language to create meaning.

  2. What are the four components of a strong literary analysis response?
    The four components are a thesis statement, SEE (Statement, Evidence, Explanation), analysis, and a clear structure with an introduction, body, and conclusion.

  3. What three things does the first line of the opening do?
    The first line makes a claim, lists specific literary techniques, and explains the effect of those techniques — this is the thesis statement.

  4. What is the Golden Rule of Tone in an analysis response?
    The Golden Rule is to analyse how the poem works, not how good the poem is.

  5. What does the second sentence of the opening provide?
    We know that the second sentence provides context — a brief overview establishing the situation and the tension that drives the poem.

  6. What does the third sentence of the opening do?
    We know that the third sentence acts as a bridge, signalling what the response will examine without going into specifics.

  7. What three things should a body paragraph statement do?
    A statement should name the technique, state its effect, and always remain connected to the thesis.

  8. What three things does the conclusion of an analysis response do?
    The conclusion circles back to restate the thesis, summarises the body paragraphs, and ends with an observation about the poem's theme.

Words that I Learnt from the Story 📚

Doughty
Meaning: Brave, valiant, resolute in character.
Example: The doughty knight rode into battle without hesitation.

Delineate
Meaning: To describe, portray, or outline with precision.
Example: The historian carefully delineated the causes of the uprising.

Intrepidity
Meaning: Fearless courage; resolute bravery.
Example: Her intrepidity in the face of danger inspired the entire team.

Ossified
Meaning: Hardened or rigid; literally turned to bone, metaphorically inflexible or stagnant.
Example: The institution’s ossified traditions resisted every attempt at reform.
